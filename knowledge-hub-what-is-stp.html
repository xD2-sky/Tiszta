What is an STP?

Knowledge Hub / STP Technologies / What is an STP?

Overview

A Sewage Treatment Plant (STP) is a facility designed to remove contaminants from sewage before the treated water is discharged to the environment or reused. The goal is to reduce organic matter, suspended solids, nutrients and pathogens to levels that meet regulatory discharge or reuse standards.

How an STP Works

Most STPs follow a similar sequence of stages, though the specific technology used at each stage varies by plant:

  • Preliminary treatment — screening and grit removal to protect downstream equipment.
  • Primary treatment — settling of suspended solids under gravity.
  • Secondary (biological) treatment — microorganisms break down dissolved and suspended organic matter. This is where technologies such as the Activated Sludge Process, SBR, MBBR and MBR differ most.
  • Secondary clarification — separating treated water from biological solids.
  • Disinfection — reducing pathogens before discharge or reuse.
  • Sludge handling — thickening, dewatering and further treatment of the solids removed at each stage.

Applications

STPs serve municipal sewage systems, industrial estates, residential townships, and standalone facilities such as hotels or hospitals. Decentralized systems (DEWATS) apply the same underlying principles at a smaller scale for communities not connected to a central sewer network.

Choosing a Technology

The right STP technology depends on flow rate, available land, effluent quality targets, and operating budget. Compact technologies like MBR deliver a smaller footprint and higher effluent quality, but typically cost more to operate than conventional processes like the Activated Sludge Process. This trade-off is explored further in our comparison of SBR and MBR.

Standards

In India, STP design is guided by CPHEEO’s Manual on Sewerage and Sewage Treatment Systems, published by the Ministry of Housing & Urban Affairs. We summarise key design norms from this manual in our CPHEEO knowledge base, alongside links to the official source.
