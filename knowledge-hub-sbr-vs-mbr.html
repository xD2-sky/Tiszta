SBR vs MBR

Knowledge Hub / STP Technologies / SBR vs MBR

Overview

Sequencing Batch Reactor (SBR) and Membrane Bioreactor (MBR) are two widely used biological treatment technologies for sewage and industrial wastewater. Both rely on microorganisms to break down organic matter, but they differ significantly in how treated water is separated from biological solids — and that difference drives most of the practical trade-offs between them.

How They Differ

SBR treats wastewater in batches within a single tank, cycling through fill, react, settle, decant and idle phases. Solids separation happens through gravity settling, similar to conventional activated sludge systems.

MBR combines biological treatment with membrane filtration, using ultrafiltration or microfiltration membranes in place of gravity settling. This produces a consistently higher-quality effluent and a smaller physical footprint, since the membrane step replaces the need for a separate clarifier.

FactorSBRMBR
Effluent qualityGoodExcellent — suitable for direct reuse in many applications
FootprintModerateSmaller — no separate clarifier required
Capital costGenerally lowerGenerally higher, due to membrane systems
Operating complexityModerateHigher — membrane fouling requires active management
Typical fitMunicipal plants, moderate land availabilitySpace-constrained sites, high reuse-quality requirements

Which One Fits Your Project?

SBR is often a strong fit where land is available and operating simplicity is a priority. MBR tends to make sense where space is limited, effluent will be reused, or discharge standards are especially stringent. The right choice always depends on site-specific flow, land, budget and reuse requirements — not a one-size-fits-all answer.
