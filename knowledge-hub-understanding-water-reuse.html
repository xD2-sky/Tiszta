Understanding Water Reuse

Knowledge Hub / Water Reuse & Recycling / Understanding Water Reuse

Overview

Water reuse means treating wastewater to a quality suitable for a beneficial purpose, rather than discharging it after a single use. As freshwater availability comes under increasing pressure, reuse is becoming a practical way for municipalities and industries to reduce dependence on freshwater sources.

The Reuse Spectrum

Water reuse spans a range of applications, generally increasing in treatment intensity as the end use gets closer to drinking water:

  • Non-potable reuse — irrigation, industrial process water, and toilet/flushing systems. This is the most common and least treatment-intensive form of reuse.
  • Indirect Potable Reuse (IPR) — highly treated water is returned to an environmental buffer, such as groundwater, before eventual withdrawal for drinking water use.
  • Direct Potable Reuse (DPR) — treated water is introduced directly into a drinking water system, without an environmental buffer, using advanced multi-barrier treatment.

Key Enabling Technologies

Reuse schemes typically combine several treatment barriers: biological treatment (often MBR, given its high effluent quality), followed by membrane filtration such as reverse osmosis, and often advanced oxidation processes for additional pathogen and micropollutant control. Zero Liquid Discharge (ZLD) systems take this further for industrial applications, aiming to recover nearly all water from a waste stream.

Why It Matters

For industrial users, reuse can reduce freshwater intake costs and improve resilience against water restrictions. For municipalities, non-potable reuse for irrigation and industrial supply can free up potable water for drinking purposes. Regulatory frameworks, including CPHEEO reuse guidelines in India, continue to evolve to support safe expansion of reuse practices.
